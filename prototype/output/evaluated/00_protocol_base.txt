Title: Isolation of Chromatin-bound Proteins from Subcellular Fractions for Biochemical Analysis

Objective: To isolate and purify chromatin-bound proteins from cellular fractions for subsequent biochemical analysis, such as Western blotting or mass spectrometry.

Materials:
- Cell culture of interest
- Phosphate-buffered saline (PBS)
- Cell scrapers or trypsin for cell detachment
- Centrifuge and microcentrifuge
- Lysis buffer (e.g., containing 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 1% SDS, 1 mM EDTA, protease inhibitors)
- Sonicator or dounce homogenizer
- High-salt wash buffer (e.g., containing 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 500 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, protease inhibitors)
- Low-salt wash buffer (e.g., containing 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 200 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, protease inhibitors)
- Elution buffer (e.g., containing 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 8.0, 1% SDS, 1 mM EDTA)
- DNase I and RNase A
- Proteinase K
- Phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1)
- Ethanol
- Glycogen (optional, as a carrier for DNA precipitation)
- Microcentrifuge tubes
- Ice and ice buckets
- Heat block or water bath

Protocol:

1. Cell Collection and Lysis:
   1.1. Harvest cells by trypsinization or scraping and collect by centrifugation at 500 x g for 5 minutes at 4°C.
   1.2. Wash the cell pellet with cold PBS and centrifuge again at 500 x g for 5 minutes at 4°C.
   1.3. Resuspend the cell pellet in lysis buffer and incubate on ice for 10 minutes.
   1.4. Lyse cells by sonication or by passing through a dounce homogenizer until the viscosity decreases, indicating chromatin shearing.

2. Isolation of Chromatin-bound Proteins:
   2.1. Centrifuge the lysate at 16,000 x g for 10 minutes at 4°C to pellet insoluble material.
   2.2. Transfer the supernatant to a new tube and discard the pellet.
   2.3. Add DNase I and RNase A to the supernatant and incubate at 37°C for 30 minutes to digest nucleic acids.
   2.4. Add Proteinase K and incubate at 55°C for 1 hour to digest proteins not bound to chromatin.

3. Chromatin Precipitation and Wash:
   3.1. Add an equal volume of phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol to the mixture and vortex vigorously.
   3.2. Centrifuge at 16,000 x g for 5 minutes at room temperature to separate phases.
   3.3. Transfer the aqueous phase to a new tube and discard the organic phase.
   3.4. Add 2-3 volumes of ice-cold ethanol and glycogen to precipitate the chromatin. Incubate at -80°C for at least 1 hour or overnight.
   3.5. Centrifuge at 16,000 x g for 20 minutes at 4°C to pellet the chromatin.
   3.6. Discard the supernatant and wash the pellet with high-salt wash buffer, followed by low-salt wash buffer, centrifuging between washes.

4. Elution of Chromatin-bound Proteins:
   4.1. Resuspend the chromatin pellet in elution buffer and incubate at 65°C for 15 minutes to reverse crosslinks.
   4.2. Centrifuge at 16,000 x g for 10 minutes at 4°C to pellet any remaining debris.
   4.3. Transfer the supernatant containing the chromatin-bound proteins to a new tube.

5. Protein Concentration Determination:
   5.1. Determine the protein concentration using a suitable assay, such as the Bradford or BCA protein assay.

6. Storage or Further Analysis:
   6.1. Store the isolated chromatin-bound proteins at -80°C for long-term storage or proceed to further biochemical analysis such as Western blotting or mass spectrometry.
